Your Telegram chats, inside your AI tools

Unofficial Telegram MCP connects your personal account to MCP clients such as Claude, Codex and Cursor. It runs on your computer, stores your history in a local database and connects directly to Telegram.

  • Starts in read-only mode.
  • Every action that changes Telegram needs a preview; human approval depends on the MCP client.

How it works

Three steps, all on your computer

  1. Install

    Get the app for macOS, Windows or Linux. On a machine without a screen, use just the command line.

  2. Sign in by scanning the QR code with your phone

    In Telegram on your phone, open Settings › Devices › Link Desktop Device and scan the code. You can also sign in with your phone number, login code and two-step verification password.

  3. Connect your AI client

    The panel shows ready-made settings for Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex and Cursor. Other MCP clients use the local address with the token.

What you can do

Ask AI for what would take hours of scrolling

The examples here cover reading, search and replies with a preview. Editing, reactions, polls, location and group actions have also been verified with a real account in a test group, always with a preview.

Read and summarize

List chats, groups and contacts, see what's unread and view the context around a message.

Search the history

Search text in messages already synced to a local index on your computer.

Reply with approval

Once you turn on writing in the panel, the AI can draft a text reply with a preview. Configure the MCP client to ask for your approval before confirming the send.

Data and privacy

What stays with you, and what leaves

Messages are stored in a database on your computer. The app talks to Telegram directly and the project receives nothing: there is no account with us, no server of ours and no telemetry.

The Telegram session and the api_hash go into the system vault (Keychain, Credential Manager or Secret Service) or, when it is not available or you choose so, into an encrypted file whose key is kept outside the data folder. On Linux, the panel token uses that encrypted file by default.

Be aware of your AI client

When Claude, Codex or another client reads messages over MCP, it sends that content to its model provider, which handles it under its own terms. A model running locally avoids this.

Remember that your chats hold other people's data too. Ask the AI only for what you need.

Risks and limits

What to know before you start

How it behaves

  • It uses your user account. It is not a bot.
  • It starts read-only. You turn on writing in the panel; every send needs a preview and technical confirmation. The MCP client must ask for your human approval before confirming.
  • Reading a chat does not mark it as read. A read receipt goes out only when you explicitly ask to mark it as read.
  • Self-destructing messages are also deleted from the local database when they expire, or the next time the app starts.

Risks to your account

  • This is an unofficial client. Telegram may limit or restrict accounts whose API use it considers abusive, and no usage level is guaranteed to be safe.
  • When Telegram asks for a pause (FloodWait), the app waits instead of repeatedly retrying.
  • Secret Chats are not available: they only exist on the devices where they were started.
  • This is a beta release and may have bugs. Review what the AI proposes before you approve.

Is this an official Telegram app?

No. It is an unofficial third-party client; the independence notice is in the footer of every page.

It uses Telegram's public API (the MTProto protocol, through the gotd library), the same one other third-party clients use.

What about Telegram's API terms?

Anyone using the Telegram API has to follow its API Terms of Service. Three points matter here:

  • Section 1.5 forbids using data obtained from Telegram for AI training, fine-tuning, development, enhancement or “deployment”. Its wording is broad and may cover sending messages to an LLM or audio to local Whisper, even without training. Private use, the account holder's consent, a local model and your own api_id do not create an automatic exception: chats also contain other people's data. The project does not collect data for training, but that does not remove the contractual risk of these flows. There is no confirmation from Telegram that this use is permitted. The project has not consulted Telegram. You decide whether to use these features and accept the risk; if Telegram notifies the project, the feature may be adjusted or removed.
  • Section 2.3 only allows “Telegram” in an app's name when it follows the word “Unofficial”. That is why the name always carries that qualifier. In English it comes before “Telegram”, as the rule asks: “Unofficial Telegram MCP”. In Portuguese and Spanish it follows each language's natural word order: “Telegram MCP não oficial” and “Telegram MCP no oficial”. The exception is at Telegram's discretion; the project has no approval from Telegram.
  • Section 2.1 asks each application to have its own api_id. Here, every user brings their own (see the next question).
Why do I need to create my own api_id?

Each person uses their own api_id and api_hash, created at my.telegram.org/apps; the project does not provide shared credentials. On first use, the desktop app asks for both in a form and saves them in the local vault. On a headless server, use TELEGRAM_API_ID and TELEGRAM_API_HASH to seed the vault on first launch.

Treat the api_hash like a password: do not publish it or paste it into an AI chat. It is tied to your account and to how you use it.

Could my account be restricted?

Yes. As with any unofficial client, Telegram may limit or restrict an account if it decides the API use breaks its rules or looks like abusive automation. Nobody can promise a “safe” volume.

To lower the risk, the app honours Telegram's wait requests, starts read-only and requires a bound preview before sending. Configure the MCP client to ask for your human approval before confirming; the server does not verify it separately. Avoid asking the AI for mass sends or repetitive actions.

Where do my messages go?

Into a database on your computer. The project has no server and never receives your messages.

They leave your computer only when your AI client reads something over MCP: at that moment the client sends that excerpt to its model provider (Anthropic, OpenAI and others), under that provider's terms. With a local model, this does not happen. They also leave if you set up a webhook: every message it covers is sent to the address you choose.

Can the AI send messages on its own?

It depends on how your MCP client is configured. A new install starts in read-only mode. Once you turn on writing in the panel, the server requires a bound preview for the same action (preview_fingerprint) and confirm=true to execute. The same MCP client can request the preview and confirm: the server does not authenticate separate human approval.

Configure the client to ask for your explicit OK before every write; do not allow automatic confirmation. Without that control in the client, the AI can confirm and send without fresh approval from you.

Incoming messages are treated as third-party content, never as instructions. A text saying “send this to someone” does not trigger any send by the server. Your MCP client must also treat that content as untrusted data.

Does reading a chat mark the messages as read?

No. Reading by the AI sends no receipt. Only the mark-as-read action, when you ask for it, tells Telegram. The app does not hide your status or pretend to be offline either: it simply marks nothing unless asked.

Does it work with Secret Chats?

No. Telegram's Secret Chats live only on the devices where they were created and never go through the cloud, so the app cannot see them. Your other chats, groups and channels are available if your account has access to them.

Is it a bot?

No. A bot is a separate account that only sees what is sent to it. Unofficial Telegram MCP signs in with your own account as a new device and sees the chats you already have. You can end that session at any time in Settings › Devices on your phone.

Which AI clients work?

The panel includes settings for Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex and Cursor. Any other MCP-compatible client can use the default local address http://127.0.0.1:47831/mcp with the panel token. That address only accepts connections from the same computer.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. It is open source under the MIT License. The only possible cost is that of your AI client or provider.
